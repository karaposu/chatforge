## Finding

### 1. The User's Intuition: Closeness at Two Levels

The user observed: "Hope and charm are so close to each other... people also hope to be charmed, and being charming usually brings sth people hope (proximity to someone attractive, power benefits)." The intuition is correct — Hope and Charm DO feel close in lived experience. The question is: at what level is the closeness real?

**Two levels of closeness exist; substrate identity does not.**

The first level of closeness is *causal correlation between structurally orthogonal variables*. High-Charm senders have status, competence, and impressiveness as resources. These resources translate into capacity to fulfill Hope-flavors — high-Charm senders make more offers (H_e fulfillment), give more attention (H_a fulfillment), validate others (H_v fulfillment), are reliable protectors (H_safe fulfillment). Selection effects amplify the correlation: receivers seeking Hope-fulfillment pursue high-Charm senders; high-Charm senders attract Hope-seeking receivers. The empirical co-occurrence of Hope and Charm in lived experience is real and strong.

The second level of closeness is *structural pattern at variable level*. Both Charm and Hope occupy the positive-valence side of a value-flow axis. Charm is current-positive value (the sender IS high-quality NOW). Hope is future-positive value (the sender WILL produce future positive outcomes). Fear is on the same axis but negative-valenced. Resonance is on a separate axis entirely (dyadic emergence, not value-flow). The shared axis between Charm and Hope explains why the user's intuition feels structural — they really do share something at variable level.

What Hope and Charm do NOT share: substrate identity. The historical-figure case is decisive. A receiver admires Napoleon's strategic competence — f_Charm is high. f_Hope is genuinely zero (Napoleon is dead; no future-positive interaction is possible; H_a/H_e/H_v/H_c/H_g/H_r/H_safe all = 0 by physical constraint). Attachment forms — historical-figure admiration is real attachment-class behavior (the receiver follows the figure, reads about them, models behavior, defends them in conversation). This shows Charm can generate attachment alone with literally zero Hope possible. Orthogonality is decisive at the substrate level.

The user's specific phenomena — "people hope to be charmed" and "being charming brings sth people hope" — both reflect the dual mechanism. The first is Object-of-Hope inclusion of Charm-related events. The second is causal correlation between Charm-resources and Hope-fulfillment-capacity. Both are empirical relationships between structurally orthogonal variables.

**On "people hope to be charmed":** Hope-instances can have Charm-related events as their OBJECT. "I hope this person will impress me" is a Hope whose object is a Charm-display event. Within iteration-3.4.1's Hope sub-flavor framework, this is H_e specialized to performance-display + H_a (attention component). It is not a separate sub-flavor (it does not pass orthogonality vs other Hope sub-flavors). It is a recognized Hope-instance configuration where the OBJECT happens to be Charm-related. iteration-3.4.1's H_s kill (Status-Hope collapsed into Charm + H_e specialized) follows the same pattern.

### 2. The Architecture's Internal Structure

iteration-3.4.2 names two structural facts about the four-variable architecture that have been implicit but never explicitly stated.

#### 2.1 Unilateral-vs-Dyadic Distinction

APT's four attachment variables split into two architectural categories:

- *Unilateral-evaluation belief-categories* (Charm, Hope, Fear) — the receiver evaluates the sender along value-flow dimensions. The variable's value is determined by the receiver's belief about the sender's properties or future actions.
- *Dyadic-emergence belief-categories* (Resonance) — both parties' world-models co-determine the variable's value. The variable's value is not determined by the receiver alone; it emerges from the relational state between both parties.

This distinction explains *why* iteration-3.2's orthogonality test fits Resonance on different grounds than Charm/Hope/Fear. Charm/Hope/Fear pass orthogonality on the unilateral-evaluation dimension (each can generate attachment alone via different evaluation flows — Charm via current-positive evaluation, Hope via future-positive expectation, Fear via threat-belief). Resonance passes orthogonality as a different *kind* of attachment variable — the dyadic shared-niche-interest case generates attachment via mutual recognition (both parties recognizing the world-model alignment), not via receiver-side evaluation alone.

Both unilateral-evaluation and dyadic-emergence categories are belief-categories under iteration-3.4's belief-frame. The unilateral-vs-dyadic distinction is at the variable-type level, not the substrate level. The substrate remains belief about the sender (iteration-3.4); the type-distinction describes how those beliefs are formed (one-directional evaluation by receiver vs co-emergent from both parties' models).

#### 2.2 Pattern A (Valence × Temporality)

Within the unilateral-evaluation domain, the three variables (Charm, Hope, Fear) occupy a recognizable structure organized by valence × temporality:

| Variable | Valence | Temporality |
|---|---|---|
| Charm | positive | current (sender IS high-quality NOW) |
| Hope | positive | future (sender WILL produce future positive outcomes) |
| Fear | negative | (mostly future — sender CAN/WILL harm) |

Resonance does NOT occupy this value-flow space. Resonance is on a separate axis (dyadic emergence) and is structurally a different *kind* of belief-category.

**Pattern A is descriptive content at variable level, NOT a substrate-reframe.** The pattern describes how the four variables are organized; it does not introduce deeper variables that generate them. The variables remain primary; the pattern is structural recognition at variable level. Cluster 4 NOT triggered (see Section 5 below).

#### 2.3 Examples

*Historical-figure case (decisive for Charm-no-Hope orthogonality):* A receiver admires Mozart's musical genius. f_Charm (admiration) is high. f_Hope is genuinely zero — Mozart is dead; no future-positive interaction is possible; all Hope sub-flavors = 0 by physical constraint. Attachment forms (the receiver "follows" Mozart, listens to recordings, defends his work in conversation, identifies with his aesthetic). This shows Charm can generate attachment alone with literally zero Hope possible — orthogonality is decisive at substrate level.

*Anonymous-offer case (borderline for Hope-no-Charm orthogonality):* A receiver gets a referral to talk to "a contact" — they know there's a concrete offer but have no information about the contact's status. f_Hope is real (specific future exchange possible); f_Charm of the contact is undetermined. The receiver may pursue the contact (engagement) but feels attached only after meeting and forming impressions. This is the borderline case: pure-Hope-zero-Charm may produce engagement-with-offer rather than attachment-to-person.

*Romantic-mentorship case (high co-occurrence; explains the user's intuition):* A receiver meets a charismatic mentor figure with high-status, competence, AND who offers concrete mentorship. f_Charm is high; f_Hope (specifically H_g + H_v + H_a from iteration-3.4.1's sub-flavors) is high. The receiver experiences attachment as a unified flow toward the sender — Charm and Hope feel inseparable. The user's "closeness" intuition reflects this experiential unity, which is empirical co-occurrence in evaluation-relevant contexts, not structural identity.

#### 2.4 Optional Cross-Discipline External Anchor

The valence × temporality distinction recurs across multiple cognitive theories at their own architectural levels:

- **Neuroscience.** Distinct brain systems for current-state evaluation (orbitofrontal cortex, ventromedial prefrontal cortex involved in reward valuation) and future-prediction (dopaminergic reward-prediction-error systems involved in anticipated value). Pattern A's two temporal axes correspond to distinct cognitive subsystems.
- **Attachment theory (Bowlby tradition).** Working models include current relational state and future projections of caregiver availability — temporal sub-structure within attachment beliefs.
- **Decision theory.** Distinguishes current asset value from discounted future value. Formal version of the temporal-valence axis.

**APT does not commit to any specific tradition's full apparatus.** Cross-discipline corroboration of Pattern A's structural form. APT does not adopt any specific neuroscience / attachment / decision-theory apparatus as canonical. Vocabulary-only overlay (parallel to iteration-3.3's optional Signal Detection Theory grounding for θ(context), iteration-3.4's optional Bayesian grounding for predictions, iteration-3.4.1's optional factor-analysis grounding for basis decomposition).

The cross-discipline corroboration is structurally important: it shows Pattern A is not APT-internal invention. It tracks a structural pattern that recurs across cognitive theories — which is what would be expected if Pattern A is descriptive content at variable level (the same pattern shows up across multiple theoretical levels) rather than a unique substrate (which would be local to APT).
