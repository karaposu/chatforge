## Cycle 1 — Broad Scan: Disambiguate the User's Phrasing

### What was scanned

The user said: "in the core of attachment there is belief? and apt is the component of significant beliefs?" Two distinct claims hide in this question:

**Claim 1:** "The core of attachment is belief." This could mean (a) attachment IS itself a belief, or (b) attachment is GENERATED BY beliefs.

**Claim 2:** "APT is the component of significant beliefs." This could mean (a) APT IS a list of beliefs that matter, or (b) APT specifies WHICH beliefs (about another person) matter for attachment.

### Disambiguation

Attachment is a behavioral disposition, not a belief. It is the receiver's tendency to engage with, persist toward, return to, the sender. APT computes this disposition from receiver-side beliefs about the sender via `f × g₁ × g₂ × g₃`. So Claim 1 reads as (b): attachment is GENERATED BY beliefs. APT does not say attachment IS a belief.

Claim 2 reads as (b): APT specifies which categories of belief about another person, when held by the receiver, produce attachment. The `f`-variables and `g`-modulators are belief-categories, not belief-tokens.

### The corrected belief-frame statement

> APT is structurally a theory of *which receiver-side beliefs about another person, when held, generate attachment*. The four `f`-variables are additive belief-categories (their values sum to drive the f-component). The three `g`-modulators are multiplicative belief-categories that gate or amplify the f-sum. The formula is the structural claim about how these belief-categories combine to produce the behavioral disposition.

### Frontier state

**Advancing.** The frame is now stated precisely. Test territory: does the frame map cleanly onto existing architecture, or does it break in load-bearing ways?

### Confidence map

- Disambiguation: **Confirmed** — frame statement now precise.
- Mapping to architecture: **Scanned** — needs probing.

All three map. The modulators are also belief-categories — but a different *kind* than the `f`-variables.

### A useful distinction emerges

- **`f`-variables = property-beliefs about sender** (what they are, what they offer, what threat they pose, what we share)
- **`g`-modulators = stance/state-beliefs about sender** (how they relate, how they cohere, how they regulate)

Both are belief-categories. But property-beliefs combine additively (the sum of properties matters); stance-beliefs combine multiplicatively (any stance failure can collapse the whole product). This is structurally meaningful: the multiplicative gating of g over f reflects that any single failure of stance reading (incoherence, emotional flooding, supplication-display) can destroy attachment regardless of how favorable the property-beliefs are.

The channel determines the threshold because different channels have different evidence-density properties (cold DM has dense weak-signal noise; warm intro has sparse high-quality evidence). The receiver's "not for me" failure read becomes: "given evidence that this person is high-SP AND no evidence that they will attend to me specifically, the belief 'they will attend to me' converges to NOT-them-attending-to-me." ## Cycle 5 — Probe Region G: Substrate-Reframe Question (Cluster 4 Test)

### What was probed

If belief-formation cognitive mechanisms (Bayesian update, schema-matching, evidence integration) are the deeper substrate of APT, does the C/H/F/R + g₁/g₂/g₃ ontology need replacement?

### The substrate test

Cluster 4 triggers when the existing ontology cannot express observed phenomena and a deeper substrate is required. The substrate test for the belief-frame: can belief-formation mechanisms *replace* the existing ontology, generating C/H/F/R + g₁/g₂/g₃ as emergent surface phenomena?

**Attempting the replacement:** Suppose we say "APT is substrate-level just Bayesian belief-update over signal evidence, and C/H/F/R + g₁/g₂/g₃ are surface taxonomies of which beliefs the update converges to." This requires specifying *which* belief categories Bayesian update converges to. Without a taxonomy, the substrate-frame is empty (it predicts that any belief category could matter, which over-generates).

**The taxonomy problem.** Belief-formation mechanism does not, by itself, predict that exactly four property-belief-categories and exactly three stance-belief-categories matter for attachment. The ontology of C/H/F/R + g₁/g₂/g₃ is not derivable from belief-formation mechanism alone; it requires additional empirical content (these specific belief-categories are the ones humans actually use to compute attachment).

**Conclusion.** Belief-formation mechanism is the *update mechanism* by which f and g values are populated; it is not a substitute for the variable+modulator ontology. The belief-frame is a META-FRAME (it explains *why* the variables are belief-categories and *how* they update), not a substrate-reframe (it does not replace them).

### Cluster 4 verdict

**NOT TRIGGERED.** The belief-frame strengthens the existing ontology rather than replacing it.

### Frontier state

**Advancing.** Beta hypothesis (substrate-reframe trigger) ruled out.

### Confidence map

- Beta (substrate reframe): **Confirmed absent** — the belief-frame is meta, not substrate.

## Cycle 6 — Probe Region H: Circularity / Self-Reference Risk (Gamma Test)

### What was probed

Does the belief-frame define beliefs in terms of what APT considers beliefs (circular), or can it be grounded externally?

### External grounding test

"Belief" in APT context = the receiver's mental representation of the sender's properties or state, formed from evidence the receiver has access to. This definition is external to APT — it draws on belief-formation cognitive theory (broadly construed: any tradition that treats beliefs as evidence-formed mental representations, including Bayesian, schema-theoretic, and attribution-theoretic traditions).

The belief-frame's claims (which categorized beliefs generate attachment when held) are then EMPIRICAL claims, not definitional ones. They could in principle be falsified by showing that a belief-category outside C/H/F/R + g₁/g₂/g₃ generates attachment, or by showing that one of those categories does not.

### Circularity verdict

**NOT CIRCULAR** when externally grounded. The risk only materializes if the spec defines "belief" by what APT happens to count as a belief — which would be circular. The fix is straightforward: in the spec section that introduces the belief-frame, anchor "belief" to the external cognitive-theory definition.

### Self-Reference Collapse failure mode (from explore reference)

The failure mode "self-reference blindness" — using a framework to evaluate something that shares assumptions with the framework — is a real risk if the belief-frame is evaluated using only APT's own concepts. Mitigation: external test cases (cognitive psychology empirical findings on belief-formation) and cross-discipline anchors (Bayesian update mechanisms, schema theory) provide non-APT grounding.

### Frontier state

**Advancing.** Gamma hypothesis (circular self-reference) ruled out *conditionally* — the frame is non-circular if and only if it is anchored externally. The spec must do this anchoring.

### Confidence map

- Gamma (circularity): **Confirmed absent conditionally** — non-circular if anchored to external belief-formation theory.

## Cycle 7 — Probe Region D + E: What Does the Frame Buy? (Predictions + Adjacent Theories)

### What does the frame BUY?

**1. Theoretical grounding.** The variable+modulator ontology was previously asserted as empirically derived (these are the belief-categories that humans use). Under the belief-frame, the ontology connects to belief-formation cognitive theory, which provides:
- A formal mechanism for f-variable updating (Bayesian-like update from evidence)
- A theoretical anchor for Resonance (schema-matching between two parties' world-models)
- A theoretical anchor for g₁ Self-Positioning (attribution of agency direction)
- A theoretical anchor for the multiplicative gating (single-source belief-failure can collapse the multi-source belief product when the source is a stance read)

**2. Sharper orthogonality test.** Iteration-3.2's orthogonality test (a putative new variable must have value independent of existing variables) was applied successfully (Resonance survived; g₄(Mode) failed in iteration-3.3). The belief-frame re-grounds the test as: "a putative new variable must be a belief-category that is not a recategorization of existing belief-categories." This gives the test a structural basis. A re-categorization of an existing belief is the same belief in different words; a genuinely new belief-category has a distinct cognitive-evidence basis.

**3. Sharper scope.**
- IN: receiver-side beliefs about sender's properties (f-variables); receiver-side stance/state-beliefs about sender (g-modulators); evidence-update mechanisms (Specificity, θ(context)); how these beliefs combine to produce attachment.
- OUT: receiver-side beliefs about self (the receiver-state pre-condition is exactly this — meta-beliefs about own availability); sender-side beliefs about anything; actual states of sender (only modeled via receiver's read); behavioral outputs of receiver other than attachment.

This is more precise than the current spec's scope statement.

**4. New predictions.** Belief-formation mechanisms predict things the current spec does not:
- *Independence-of-sources prediction:* if multiple independent evidence sources signal the same direction (warm intro + social proof + reputation all signal high f_Charm), the belief update is faster than if a single source signals (or if multiple sources are correlated). PRAGMA could test this.
- *Prior-strength prediction:* a strong f_prior is slow to update by single in-interaction signals; weak f_prior is fast to update. This refines iteration-3.3's f_prior treatment with update-rate predictions.
- *Evidence-quality threshold prediction:* low-quality evidence (specificity below θ) does not update beliefs even if frequent. The threshold is binary, not graded. iteration-3.3's Refinement 2 already states this; the belief-frame gives it cognitive-theory grounding.

**5. Pedagogical clarity.** A new reader of APT can understand the formula's structure faster under the belief-frame ("these are the receiver's beliefs about the sender, weighted and combined to produce attachment") than under the bare formula. The pedagogical anchor is significant for an architecture that has accumulated complexity across five iterations.

### Adjacent theory connections

- **Bayesian belief-update:** evidence-quality threshold; prior-weighted update; multi-source independence. Mechanism for f-variable updating.
- **Schema theory:** schema-matching as the cognitive operation underlying Resonance. Anchors the dyadic-relational variable.
- **Attribution theory:** attribution of agency direction (toward-self vs toward-other) as the cognitive operation underlying g₁ Self-Positioning.
- **Bowlby attachment theory:** working models of relational availability; the belief-frame puts APT in dialogue with the parent-child attachment tradition.
- **Adult attachment theory (anxious/avoidant/secure):** receiver-side patterns of belief-formation about partners. APT generalizes/specifies the belief-formation patterns.

The connections are *open* — APT can borrow vocabulary and findings from these traditions without committing to their full theoretical apparatus. (Same constraint as iteration-3.3's optional SDT theoretical grounding for Refinement 2: vocabulary import, not framework import.)

### Frontier state

**Advancing.** Region D + E confirmed.

### Confidence map

- Region D (predictions added): **Confirmed** — at least three new prediction classes
- Region E (adjacent theories): **Confirmed** — multiple compatible connections

## Cycle 8 — Probe Region F: What's NOT a Belief in APT? (Boundary)

### What was probed

For the belief-frame to be useful, it must distinguish what is and isn't a belief in APT. If everything is a belief, the frame says nothing.

| f-variables (C/H/F/R) | YES — receiver-side property-beliefs about sender | |
| g-modulators (SP/Coherence/EC) | YES — receiver-side stance-beliefs about sender | |
| Specificity (signal property) | NO — quality measure of evidence, not a belief itself | |
| θ(context) | NO — evidence-quality threshold, not a belief; a parameter of the update mechanism | |
| The formula `f × g₁ × g₂ × g₃` | NO — structural claim about how beliefs combine, not a belief itself | |
| Channel | NO — environmental fact (where the interaction happens) | |
| Receiver availability (pre-condition) | YES, but a meta-belief about self, not about sender | |
| Attachment magnitude/type (output) | NO — behavioral disposition derived from beliefs | |
| Sender's actual state | NO — APT doesn't model this; only receiver's read | |

### The clean boundary

Under the belief-frame:
- **Beliefs about sender** = inside formula (f and g)
- **Beliefs about self** = outside formula (pre-condition)
- **Properties of evidence** = update-mechanism parameters (Specificity, θ)
- **Properties of channel** = update-mechanism parameters (channel sets θ)
- **Combining structure** = the formula itself
- **Output** = behavioral disposition

This boundary is sharper than what the current spec provides. The receiver-state pre-condition, in particular, is now structurally explained: it is a belief, but not a belief about the sender, so it is outside APT's formula by category, not by stipulation.

### Direction: Deliberately different

Scan in a region not previously considered: economic and decision-theoretic models of partner selection and relationship formation. These include rational choice theory of mate selection, match theory in marriage markets, and utility-maximization models of relationship investment.

### What was found

These models assume known utilities or revealed preferences, not formed beliefs. They model partner choice as utility-maximization given partner traits, not as belief-update over signals about traits. The closest cognitive analogue is *bounded rationality* / *behavioral economics*, which acknowledges heuristic and noisy belief-formation — but the dominant frame in economics is "agents have preferences over states" not "agents form beliefs about partners."

### Implication for the belief-frame

The economic frame is structurally different. APT's belief-frame is closer to behavioral economics / cognitive psychology than to rational choice. This is informative for theoretical positioning: APT belongs to the cognitive-theoretic tradition of relationship modeling, not the decision-theoretic tradition.
