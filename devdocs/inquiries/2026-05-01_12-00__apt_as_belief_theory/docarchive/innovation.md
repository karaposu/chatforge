## Direction (Intuition Component)

**Context:** Five iterations of APT have established the variable + modulator + specificity architecture; iteration-3.3 just shipped three pre-content refinements; the user pulled on Refinement 1 to ask whether APT structurally is a theory of attachment-generating beliefs.

**Valuation:** Getting the frame statement right matters most — it sets the tone for whether the frame reads as theoretical grounding or as restatement. Getting the property-vs-stance distinction articulated cleanly matters next — it's the new structural insight that distinguishes this iteration from "merely renaming." Getting external anchoring right (vocabulary-only, non-committal) protects against Self-Reference Collapse without committing to specific apparatus. Getting iteration-label discipline right (4-category convention extension) keeps the spec history navigable.

**Motivation:** Iteration-3.4 should ship as a content-bearing meta-frame that future iterations can lean on, not as a ceremonial restatement.

### P1 — Frame foundation

#### P1-A (generic) — "Minimal frame statement + one-paragraph anchor + brief positioning" [Constraint Manipulation]

**Mechanism:** Constraint Manipulation. Constraint: stay vocabulary-only; no apparatus commitment; one paragraph each for frame statement, anchor, positioning.

**Output:**
- *Frame statement:* "APT specifies which receiver-side beliefs about another person, when held, generate attachment. The four `f`-variables (Charm, Hope, Fear, Resonance) are additive *property-belief* categories. The three `g`-modulators (Self-Positioning, Coherence, Emotional Composure) are multiplicative *stance-belief* categories. The formula `f × g₁ × g₂ × g₃` is the structural claim about how these belief-categories combine to produce a behavioral disposition (attachment)."
- *Disambiguation:* "Two readings of the user's framing are possible. First: 'attachment IS belief.' Second: 'attachment is GENERATED BY beliefs.' APT says the second. Attachment is a behavioral disposition — the receiver's tendency to engage with, persist toward, return to the sender. APT computes this disposition from receiver-side beliefs about the sender. Similarly: APT does not list specific beliefs (it does not say 'believe X about person Y'); it specifies which *categories* of belief about another person are load-bearing for attachment-generation."
- *External anchor:* "APT's account of how f-values and g-values are populated borrows vocabulary from belief-formation cognitive theory broadly construed — Bayesian belief-update, schema theory, attribution theory, and the working-models tradition descending from Bowlby. APT does not commit to any specific tradition's full apparatus; compatibility with multiple traditions is treated as a feature, not a problem to resolve. Tradition-specific apparatus may be imported case-by-case (parallel to iteration-3.3's optional Signal Detection Theory grounding for the context-dependent threshold) but no tradition is canonical."
- *Theoretical positioning:* "APT belongs to the cognitive-theoretic tradition of relationship modeling (Bowlby, adult attachment theory, schema-based approaches) rather than the decision-theoretic tradition (rational choice, match theory, utility-maximization). Within the cognitive-theoretic tradition, APT's contribution is structural: a specific set of belief-categories with a specific combination structure (additive `f` × multiplicative `g`) that produces attachment as output."

#### P1-B (focused) — "Frame + anchor + positioning unified into one 'Theoretical Grounding' introduction" [Combination]

**Mechanism:** Combination. Frame statement + external anchor + theoretical positioning unified into a single integrated "Theoretical Grounding" introduction section.

**Output:** A single ~3-paragraph introduction that flows: (1) state the frame; (2) anchor it externally with vocabulary-only disclaimer; (3) position APT's contribution within the cognitive-theoretic tradition. Each paragraph carries the previous paragraph's load-bearing terms forward (frame statement names "belief-categories"; anchor explains where the term comes from; positioning shows what the frame's specific contribution is). The unified introduction reads as a single argumentative arc rather than three separate notes.

**Why combination:** The three sub-elements (frame / anchor / positioning) cohere thematically; combining them produces a stronger introduction than three isolated paragraphs.

#### P1-C (contrarian) — "Negative-space framing: APT specifies which beliefs DO NOT generate attachment" [Inversion]

**Mechanism:** Inversion. Invert the frame's polarity: "APT specifies which beliefs DO NOT generate attachment" rather than "APT specifies which beliefs DO."

**Output:** "APT specifies the structural boundary between attachment-generating and attachment-irrelevant beliefs. Beliefs about a person's hair color, height, ZIP code, favorite music — most beliefs about another person — do not generate attachment. APT names the small set that does (Charm, Hope, Fear, Resonance, Self-Positioning, Coherence, Emotional Composure) and explains why others do not."

**Inversion depth check:** First-level inversion (positive → negative). The negative-space framing has rhetorical power but loses precision: it would require enumerating non-attachment-generating beliefs (an open-ended set) to be useful. Goes deeper: "APT defines attachment-generating beliefs by combinatorial closure (the four-plus-three structure) rather than by enumeration of inclusions or exclusions." This is the system-level inversion — the frame's claim is structural, not enumerative. The system-level inversion converges back to the positive framing because that's where the structural claim lives.

### P2 — Property-vs-stance belief distinction

#### P2-A (generic) — "Distinction stated + structural justification + worked example" [Combination]

**Mechanism:** Combination. Property-beliefs + stance-beliefs + combination-structure → unified distinction.

**Output:**
- *Distinction:* "APT's belief-categories split into two kinds. Property-beliefs about the sender (what they are: status, threat, future-promise, world-model match) populate the `f`-variables. Stance/state-beliefs about the sender (how they are: their agency-direction, their internal coherence, their emotional regulation) populate the `g`-modulators."
- *Structural justification:* "Property-beliefs sum independently because each property contributes to attachment-generation independently of the others — being high-status, being a future-positive prospect, being a threat to navigate, sharing world-models, are independent attachment generators that add. Stance-beliefs gate the product because any single stance-failure (incoherence read, emotional flooding read, supplication-display read) destroys the receiver's ability to form a stable belief about the sender — and without a stable belief, attachment cannot consolidate regardless of how favorable the property-beliefs are."
- *Worked example:* "A sender with high property-beliefs across the board (genuine Charm + Hope + Resonance) but a single stance-failure (Coherence collapse from contradictory behavior over time) generates attachment that decays — demonstrating multiplicative gating: the receiver had favorable beliefs about the sender's properties but lost the ability to model the sender stably. Contrast: a sender with one strong property-belief (Resonance only — shared niche interest) and minimal others, with all stance-readings clean, generates real attachment — demonstrating additive sum: a single property-belief is sufficient when stance-beliefs don't gate it down."

#### P2-B (focused) — "Naming what was implicit since iteration-3.2" [Absence Recognition]

**Mechanism:** Absence Recognition. Iteration-3.2 made the architectural decision that `f` is additive and `g` is multiplicative without explaining *why* the kind-difference warrants different combination structures. The reasoning was empirical (Resonance can generate attachment alone; Coherence-collapse can destroy attachment from any starting state). Under the belief-frame, the empirical observations are explained by the property-vs-stance distinction.

**Output:** Frame the section as "What iteration-3.2 noticed but did not name." iteration-3.2's structural classification (Resonance as 4th attachment variable, additive in f; Modulator Suite, multiplicative in g) was driven by the orthogonality test (can it generate attachment alone? can its failure collapse attachment alone?). The orthogonality test was implicitly distinguishing property-beliefs (which generate independently) from stance-beliefs (which gate the whole). The belief-frame names what the orthogonality test was implicitly tracking.

**Why absence recognition:** Iteration-3.2's content was correct; what was missing was the *explanation* of why the test produced the structure it did. The distinction fills that absence.

#### P2-C (contrarian) — "Property-vs-stance is the wrong axis; should be object-vs-process" [Inversion]

**Mechanism:** Inversion. Challenge that property-vs-stance is the right distinction.

**Output:** Alternative framing: f-variables represent OBJECT-beliefs (the receiver's beliefs about the sender as an object of evaluation: their status, prospects, threat, alignment); g-modulators represent PROCESS-beliefs (the receiver's beliefs about the sender as an active agent: their agency direction, their internal-state stability, their emotional regulation in process). The distinction maps approximately onto property-vs-stance but carries different intuitions — object-vs-process is more about *what is being modeled* (entity vs activity) than *what kind of attribute* (static property vs dynamic stance).

**Inversion depth check:** First-level. Going deeper: "every belief is about something, so object-vs-process collapses into all beliefs being about both" — which doesn't help. Stay at first level. The contrarian carries some appeal (it foregrounds the temporal dimension implicit in stance-beliefs) but adds vocabulary without clearly improving on property-vs-stance.

### P3 — Three new predictions + PRAGMA implementability

#### P3-A (generic) — "Forward-extrapolated predictions table + cost profiles + test sketches" [Extrapolation]

**Mechanism:** Extrapolation. Project the frame forward to predict empirical patterns the existing iterations cannot.

**Output:**

| Prediction | Statement | PRAGMA cost | Test sketch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-source independence | If multiple independent evidence sources signal the same f-variable direction (e.g., social proof + reputation + warm-intro all signal high Charm), the f-update is faster than if a single source signals or if multiple sources are correlated. | Medium (new classifier for source-correlation needed) | Hold f_prior at zero; vary number-and-independence of evidence sources signaling same direction; measure update rate. |
| Prior-strength | Strong f_prior (well-established prior belief from rich history of evidence) is slow to update by single in-interaction signals; weak f_prior is fast. | Low (extends iter-3.3 R1 treatment) | Establish f_prior at high vs zero via warm-intro + social-proof manipulation; deliver same single in-interaction signal; compare attachment-formation rate. |
| Evidence-quality threshold | Low-specificity evidence (specificity < θ(context)) does not update beliefs even if frequent. | Zero (re-grounding only — already in iter-3.3 R2) | Already specified in iter-3.3; no new test required; the frame just grounds the existing prediction. |

**Why extrapolation:** Each prediction projects the belief-update mechanism's implications onto observable patterns.

#### P3-B (focused) — "Bayesian belief-update formalism for the predictions" [Domain Transfer]

**Mechanism:** Domain Transfer from Bayesian belief-update.

**Output:** Port Bayesian update mechanism's structural primitives (prior, likelihood, posterior, evidence-independence assumption) as vocabulary anchors for the three predictions:
- *Multi-source independence prediction* corresponds to Bayes' assumption that independent evidence updates posterior multiplicatively (combining likelihoods independently); correlated evidence updates less than its raw count suggests.
- *Prior-strength prediction* corresponds to the strength of the prior in Bayes' update: a strong prior (many prior observations) requires more evidence to shift than a weak prior.
- *Evidence-quality threshold prediction* corresponds to a likelihood-cutoff: evidence below a likelihood threshold is treated as non-informative and does not update.

The frame uses Bayesian vocabulary as one of several tradition-specific anchors (per the vocabulary-only commitment) — adopting the structural concepts without committing to numerical Bayesian implementation.

**Why domain transfer:** Bayesian update has 250+ years of formalism for prior-update-evidence triple. Importing the structural concepts (not the formalism) gives the predictions a recognizable shape for readers from quantitative cognitive science backgrounds.

#### P3-C (contrarian) — "Predictions are circular; the frame doesn't actually predict anything new" [Inversion]

**Mechanism:** Inversion. Challenge the frame's prediction-bearing claim.

**Output:** "The three predictions are not new content. (1) Multi-source independence is just a special case of additive `f` — independent sources signaling the same direction add their contributions, which iteration-3.2.1 already established as additive. (2) Prior-strength is just iteration-3.3 Refinement 1 with extra steps — saying 'strong prior is slow to update' is the same as saying 'f_prior contributes substantially.' (3) Evidence-quality threshold is iteration-3.3 Refinement 2 verbatim. The frame produces zero novel predictions; it just relabels existing content with belief-formation vocabulary."

**Inversion depth check:** First-level. The contrarian case has surface plausibility — predictions 2 and 3 do indeed connect to existing iteration content. The contest: does prediction 1 (multi-source independence with source-correlation detection requirement) reduce to additive `f` alone, or does it add something? It adds: additive `f` does not say anything about whether independent sources update faster than correlated sources. Multi-source independence is a NEW claim about update rate as a function of source-correlation, and it requires NEW PRAGMA classifier work (source-correlation detection). Predictions 2 and 3 are re-groundings of existing predictions, granted; prediction 1 is genuinely new.
